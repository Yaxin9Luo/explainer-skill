# ASD-STE100 for explanations

ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) is a controlled-language spec from aerospace maintenance documentation. Its value for explanations: every sentence carries one idea, with no ambiguity.

## Core rules (keep these on, always)

1. **One idea per sentence.** One instruction per sentence in procedures.
2. **Sentence length.** Procedural ≤ 20 words. Descriptive ≤ 25 words.
3. **Paragraph length.** ≤ 6 sentences. Topic sentence first.
4. **Active voice** by default. Imperative for instructions ("Compute the ratio.", not "The ratio should be computed.").
5. **Simple tenses**: present, past, future. No "would have been".
6. **One term, one meaning.** Pick a name for a thing and never swap synonyms ("policy" stays "policy", not "actor" two lines later).
7. **No noun stacks** longer than three words ("clipped surrogate objective" is fine; "PPO clipped surrogate objective gradient estimate" is not — break it up).
8. **Keep articles** (a, an, the). Do not write telegraphic text.
9. **Vertical lists** for steps and for three or more parallel items.
10. **Say the conclusion before the reason.**

Formulas, code, and variable lists are not sentences: they do not count toward the length limits. Put them on their own line, then explain them in short sentences.

Check a written answer before sending it: `python3 $S/scripts/explainer.py lint answer.md` lists sentences over 25 words (`--max 20` for procedures). Chinese and Japanese are counted in characters (limit 45, `--max-cjk`). Split what it flags.

## Full STE vs. "80% STE" (default)

Full STE also requires the STE dictionary: only approved words, each with one approved meaning, plus no -ing forms and almost no passive. That is too stiff for research topics.

In 80% mode, relax only these:
- Normal technical and academic vocabulary is fine. Define a term once on first use.
- -ing forms are fine ("clipping the ratio").
- Passive is fine when the actor is unknown or irrelevant ("the gradient is set to zero").
- Descriptive sentences may reach ~30 words when a formula is inline.

If the user asks for "full STE" or "strict STE", turn the dictionary-style restrictions back on: common short words, one meaning each, no -ing, no passive.

## Example (80%)

Before: "PPO, which was introduced as a simpler alternative to TRPO, essentially works by constraining how much the policy can change, and it does this through a clipping mechanism that is applied to the probability ratio."

After:
PPO limits how much the policy changes in one update. It computes the ratio r = π_new(a|s) / π_old(a|s). It then clips r to the range [1−ε, 1+ε]. The objective uses the smaller of the clipped and unclipped terms. This removes the incentive to move the policy far from the old one.
